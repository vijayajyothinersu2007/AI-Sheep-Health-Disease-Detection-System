## Importance of Housing Sanitation
Over 70% of endemic infectious diseases in small ruminants (including foot rot, sheep pox, coccidiosis, and enterotoxemia) are directly linked to poor housing hygiene, damp bedding, and high ammonia accumulation. Routine disinfection breaks pathogen cycles and minimizes tick, fly, and mite vectors.

## Daily Sanitation Schedule
1. **Dung and Wet Bedding Removal:** Scrape and clear accumulated droppings every morning before letting sheep out to graze. Do not allow manure to form slurry in loafing areas.
2. **Water Trough Cleaning:** Scrub drinking troughs daily using a stiff brush to remove algae and biofilm. Drain and refill with fresh, potable water.
3. **Feed Manger Clearing:** Empty leftover unconsumed fodder daily to avoid fungal mold (aflatoxins) and fermentation.

## Weekly and Monthly Disinfection Protocols
- **Limewashing:** Apply quicklime (calcium oxide) wash with 1-2% copper sulfate or 0.5% bleaching powder to wooden partitions, stone walls, and floor areas twice a month.
- **Bleaching Powder Sprinkling:** Spread bleaching powder (calcium hypochlorite) around boundary fences, water points, and shed entrances.
- **Formalin or Glutaraldehyde Spray:** During disease outbreaks, spray empty quarantine sheds with 1-2% formalin or approved veterinary glutaraldehyde disinfectants. Ensure animals are kept out until completely dry and ventilated (minimum 24 hours).
- **Flame Gun Treatment:** Use LPG blowtorches/flame guns on stone or dirt floors to incinerate resistant coccidial oocysts and external parasite eggs.

## Manure Composting & Vector Control
- Manure should be dumped in a composting pit located at least 50 meters away downwind from the animal shed.
- Deep pit composting generates high internal temperatures (55-65°C) that destroy worm eggs, larva, and pathogens within 3 to 4 weeks.
- Apply fly repellent sprays (cypermethrin/deltamethrin 1%) to manure heaps to prevent housefly and stable fly breeding.
