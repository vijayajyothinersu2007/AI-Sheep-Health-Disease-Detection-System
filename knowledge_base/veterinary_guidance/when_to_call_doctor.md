## Veterinary Clinical Referral Decision Tree
While basic supportive care and isolation can be initiated by the shepherd, delay in professional veterinary medical intervention is the leading cause of flock mortality.

## Normal Sheep Vital Parameters (Baseline Reference)
- **Rectal Temperature:** 102.0°F – 103.5°F (38.8°C – 39.7°C)
- **Respiration Rate:** 15 – 30 breaths per minute (at rest)
- **Heart / Pulse Rate:** 70 – 90 beats per minute
- **Rumen Motility:** 1 – 2 contractions every 2 minutes (palpated at left flank)

## Red Flag Scenarios Requiring IMMEDIATE Doctor Referral (< 2 Hours)
1. **Severe High Fever or Subnormal Temperature:**
   - Temperature > 105.0°F (40.5°C) indicates acute systemic septicemia (Domma, Anthrax, PPR).
   - Subnormal temperature (< 100°F / 37.8°C) in a recumbent sheep indicates shock or terminal toxemia.
2. **Acute Respiratory Distress & Frothing:**
   - Animal extending neck, breathing with open mouth, gasping, or foaming blood/mucus from nostrils.
3. **Severe Abdominal Distension (Acute Bloat):**
   - Left flank tensely swollen like a drum, animal groaning and unable to stand.
4. **Dystocia (Difficult Lambing):**
   - Ewe in active hard labor for over 45 minutes with no lamb delivered, or abnormal presentation (head only, tail only).
5. **Neurological Symptoms / Opisthotonos:**
   - Circling, head pressing, muscle tremors, convulsions, or head thrown backwards (Enterotoxemia / Listeriosis).
6. **Sudden Multiple Deaths in Flock:**
   - More than 1 sheep dying within 24 hours without prolonged illness. Suspect anthrax or hemorrhagic septicemia—do NOT open carcass.

## Yellow Flag Scenarios Requiring Routine Referral (< 24 Hours)
- Progressive lameness in multiple animals with foul foot odor.
- Profuse watery diarrhea or bloody stools for > 12 hours.
- Chronic weight loss with bottle jaw (submandibular edema).
- Extensive skin pox eruptions, pustules, or scabs spreading across face and inner legs.
