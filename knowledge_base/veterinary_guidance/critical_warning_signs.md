## Red-Flag Emergency Indicators: Call a Veterinarian Immediately

The presence of any of the following clinical indicators represents an acute medical or surgical emergency. Home care is dangerous in these scenarios; transport the sheep immediately to the nearest veterinary hospital or summon a field veterinarian.

### 1. Severe Acute Respiratory Distress & Throat Obstruction
- **Signs:** Open-mouthed gasping, loud raspy wheezing/stridor, neck stretched forward, bluish (cyanotic) tongue, intense hot swelling of the throat and neck.
- **Underlying Crisis:** Acute Domma / Haemorrhagic Septicaemia, foreign body choking, or severe pulmonary Sheep Pox.
- **Action:** DO NOT drench any liquid orally. Keep sheep calm and upright in a shaded pen. Seek emergency antibiotic and airway therapy.

### 2. Acute Neurological Crisis & Convulsions
- **Signs:** Head arched stiffly backward over the spine (opisthotonos), continuous teeth grinding, blind staggering or running into walls, violent leg paddling, rapid muscle tremors.
- **Underlying Crisis:** Enterotoxemia (Pulpy Kidney), acute organophosphate pesticide poisoning, tetanus, or cerebrocortical necrosis (polioencephalomalacia).
- **Action:** Protect animal from hitting sharp walls or water troughs. Administer no home drenches.

### 3. Acute Severe Rumen Tympany (Frothy Bloat)
- **Signs:** Left flank severely distended like a drum, tight and drum-like when tapped, sheep groaning, unable to lie down, rapid distressed breathing.
- **Underlying Crisis:** Starch or green legume bloat causing diaphragm compression and suffocation.
- **Action:** Immediate veterinary trocharization or stomach tube decompression; administer bloat drench (simethicone / vegetable oil) under emergency guidance.

### 4. Severe Blood Loss or Tarry Fluid Discharge
- **Signs:** Profuse dark tarry diarrhoea with straining and groaning; bleeding from rectum, nose, or vulva; extreme eye pallor (paper-white conjunctiva).
- **Underlying Crisis:** Heavy acute Haemonchus infestation, coccidiosis, severe enteritis, or anthrax.

### 5. Acute Maggot Wound (Deep Myiasis)
- **Signs:** Deep necrotic cavitating wound on hoof, horn base, or tail infested with hundreds of writhing larvae, bleeding, intense pain, and foul odor.
- **Underlying Crisis:** Risk of systemic bacterial toxaemia, septicaemia, and death.
