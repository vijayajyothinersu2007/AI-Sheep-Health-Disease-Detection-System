# First Aid for Acute Ruminal Tympany (Bloat) in Sheep

## What is Bloat?
Bloat is the rapid accumulation of gas in the sheep rumen (reticulorumen). Because the sheep cannot eructate (belch), the distended rumen compresses the diaphragm, lungs, and vena cava, leading to death by asphyxiation and heart failure within 30 to 60 minutes.

## Recognizing Bloat Severity
- **Signs:** Severe swelling of the left flank (drum-like when tapped), kicking at the belly, grinding teeth, mouth open with tongue protruding, frantic bleating, and laboured breathing.
- Left paralumbar fossa extends higher than the spine.

## Immediate Farm First Aid Steps
1. **Remove From Pasture Immediately:** Move all sheep away from lush green legumes (alfalfa, clover, young green wheat).
2. **Elevate Front Legs:** Position the sheep with its front quarters elevated on a mound or slope to shift rumen contents away from the cardia.
3. **Wooden Bit in Mouth:** Place a smooth wooden stick across the mouth (like a horse bit) secured with string behind the ears. This encourages continuous chewing, salivation, and natural belching.
4. **Administration of Anti-Foaming Agents (For Frothy Bloat):**
   - Administer 50 to 100 ml of vegetable cooking oil (groundnut, sunflower, or mustard oil) orally via drenching bottle slowly to avoid aspiration into lungs.
   - Alternatively, administer 50 to 100 ml of liquid paraffin or veterinary Bloatosil / Dimethicone / Simethicone suspension.
   - The oil breaks the surface tension of foam bubbles, releasing free gas.
5. **Stomach Tube Passing:** A veterinarian or trained livestock assistant can pass a flexible rubber stomach tube (1/2 inch outer diameter) lubricated with oil through the mouth into the rumen to relieve free gas.

## Extreme Emergency Trocarization
- When the animal is gasping, recumbent, and within minutes of death:
- Puncture the center of the left paralumbar fossa (midway between the last rib and the hip bone) using a sterile veterinary trocar and cannula or large 14-gauge needle.
- Release gas gradually. Never perform this without veterinary supervision unless all other measures have failed and death is imminent.
