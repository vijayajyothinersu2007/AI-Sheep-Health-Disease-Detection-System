## Principles of Small Ruminant Wound Care
Open cuts, shearing wounds, predator bites, and castration wounds in sheep are highly vulnerable to bacterial tetanus, clostridial infections, and blowfly maggot strike (myiasis).

## Step-by-Step Emergency Wound Protocol
1. **Restrain and Clean Hands:** Secure the sheep gently on clean straw. Wash hands with soap and water or wear disposable gloves.
2. **Hemorrhage Control (Bleeding):** Apply firm, continuous pressure directly over the bleeding site with a clean sterile gauze or cloth for 3 to 5 minutes. Do NOT repeatedly lift the cloth to check clotting.
3. **Hair Trimming:** Carefully clip wool/hair around the wound margins for at least 2 inches using rounded shears. Prevent loose hair from falling into the wound.
4. **Flushing & Decontamination:**
   - Flush the wound copiously with sterile normal saline (0.9%) or clean boiled lukewarm water with 1 teaspoon salt per liter.
   - Dilute Potassium Permanganate (light pink 1:1000) can be used to flush mud and debris.
   - Do NOT pour strong hydrogen peroxide or concentrated Dettol/phenol directly into deep wounds as it destroys healthy tissue granulation.
5. **Antiseptic Dressing:**
   - Apply 5% Povidone-Iodine ointment or liquid over the clean wound.
   - Apply veterinary fly-repellent spray (Gamma benzene hexachloride, eucalyptus/neem/turpentine formulation) around and on the perimeter of the wound to repel blowflies.
6. **Maggot Wound Treatment (Myiasis):**
   - If maggots are visible, soak clean cotton in eucalyptus oil, turpentine oil, or chloroform-ether mixture and plug the cavity for 5-10 minutes.
   - Remove suffocated maggots using clean forceps. Flush with saline and dress with povidone-iodine.
7. **Tetanus Prophylaxis:** Inquire with veterinarian for Tetanus Toxoid or anti-tetanus serum (ATS) administration, especially for deep puncture or dog bite wounds.
