## Disease Overview
Domma, known scientifically as Haemorrhagic Septicaemia (HS) or Pasteurellosis, is an acute, peracute, highly lethal bacterial septicaemic disease caused primarily by *Pasteurella multocida* (serotypes B:2 and E:2). It is one of the most fatal infectious diseases affecting small and large ruminants in India, particularly during the onset of the monsoon and extreme weather shifts. The bacteria produce endotoxins that trigger massive throat oedema, severe toxaemia, circulatory collapse, and suffocation within 12 to 36 hours if untreated.

## Clinical Symptoms & Signs
- **Peracute Cases:** Sudden death without preceding clinical signs, often occurring overnight in apparently healthy, vigorous sheep.
- **Acute Cases:**
  - High fever (105°F to 107°F / 40.5°C to 41.7°C), severe shivering, depression, and drooped ears.
  - Submandibular and brisket oedema: Marked, hot, painful, tense swelling of the submandibular region, throat, neck, and dewlap.
  - Respiratory distress: Frothy salivation, open-mouth gasping, loud wheezing/stridor, cyanotic (bluish) mucous membranes due to throat compression.
  - Tongue protrusion: The tongue is frequently swollen and protruded from the mouth.
  - Gastrointestinal signs: Early constipation followed by severe bloody diarrhoea or dysentery, collapse, and hypothermic coma.

## Transmission & Spread
- The causative organism, *Pasteurella multocida*, often resides as a harmless commensal in the upper respiratory tract of carrier sheep.
- Heavy triggers: Sudden onset of rains, transport fatigue, overcrowding, malnutrition, and exposure to cold/chilling dampen mucosal immunity, allowing the bacterium to rapidly proliferate and invade the bloodstream.
- Spread occurs via ingestion of feed and water contaminated with nasal discharges and saliva of sick animals, as well as inhalation of droplet aerosols in confined night sheds.

## Veterinary Medical Treatment
- **Emergency Priority:** Early intervention within the first 6 to 12 hours of fever before extensive throat oedema occurs is the only chance of survival.
- **Urgent Antibiotic Therapy (Prescribed by Veterinarian Only):**
  - High-dose broad-spectrum parenterals: Sulfadimidine (33.3% solution at 100-150 mg/kg IV/SC), or Oxytetracycline (10-15 mg/kg IM/IV), or Ceftiofur sodium (1-2 mg/kg IM), or Enrofloxacin (5 mg/kg IM).
- **Anti-inflammatory & Decongestive Therapy:**
  - Intravenous or intramuscular Dexamethasone or Meloxicam to rapidly relieve severe throat and tracheal inflammatory swelling that is blocking respiration.
- **Fluid & Shock Management:**
  - Intravenous fluid therapy (Dextrose Normal Saline, Ringer's Lactate) to combat endotoxic shock, dehydration, and circulatory failure.
- **Airway Support:**
  - Emergency tracheostomy may be required by a qualified veterinarian if throat swelling completely closes the laryngeal passage.
- **Veterinary Warning:** Self-injecting medications or delaying veterinary contact is almost uniformly fatal. Medications must be confirmed and administered by a veterinarian.

## Safe Supportive & Home Care
- **Quiet, Shaded Isolation:** Move affected sheep immediately into a dry, warm, calm pen with plenty of fresh air. Minimize all handling and noise, as stress precipitously accelerates respiratory failure.
- **Hydration Support:** Keep fresh, clean, cool water available at all times.
- **DO NOT FORCE DRENCH:** Never drench liquids or medicine orally with a drenching bottle or pipe when the sheep is gasping or has throat swelling. Drenching in this state will cause immediate aspiration pneumonia and death.
- **Cool Compresses on Swelling:** Apply cool, clean wet cloths gently around the throat area to soothe heat and tension while awaiting the veterinarian.
- **Home Care Warning:** Home care is strictly auxiliary. HS cannot be cured by herbs, salt, or home concoctions; immediate emergency veterinary antibiotic therapy is imperative.

## Prevention & Vaccination
- **Pre-Monsoon Vaccination:** Annual vaccination with Haemorrhagic Septicaemia alum-precipitated or oil-adjuvant vaccine, administered 1-2 months before the onset of monsoon rains (May-June).
- **Quarantine:** Isolate newly introduced sheep for at least 21 days before joining the main flock.
- **Stress Reduction:** Avoid long trekking of sheep during rainy or excessively cold periods; ensure night shelters are dry and free of cold draughts.
- **Trough Sanitization:** Disinfect feed and water troughs weekly with 1% potassium permanganate or bleaching powder.
- **Proper Carcass Disposal:** HS carcasses contain massive numbers of viable bacteria in blood and tissues. Never open or skin the carcass. Bury deep under 6 feet of soil covered with quicklime.

## Emergency Warning Signs
Seek emergency veterinary intervention IMMEDIATELY if:
- Sheep develops throat swelling with loud raspy breathing.
- Tongue is protruding and purple/blue.
- Body temperature exceeds 105°F and sheep is trembling or unable to stand.
