## Clinical Overview
Excessive Salivation (Ptyalism / Drooling / Sialorrhea) is **NOT a single disease**; it is a critical clinical sign indicating an underlying irritation, ulceration, physical obstruction, systemic infection, or chemical/plant toxicosis affecting the oral cavity, pharynx, or central nervous system. Because several major contagious diseases and poisonings present with excessive frothy or ropy salivation, determining the precise underlying etiology is essential.

## Primary Differential Diagnoses & Causes
1. **Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD):** Highly contagious viral disease (Aphthovirus) producing vesicles and painful erosions on the dental pad, tongue, and gums, accompanied by severe foot lesions and lip smacking.
2. **Contagious Ecthyma (Orf):** Parapoxvirus causing proliferative crusts and ulcers around the lips, gums, and tongue.
3. **Foreign Body / Trauma:** Sharp wire, thorns (acacia thorns, prosopis thorns), burrs, or sharp grass seeds lodged in gums, dental arcade, or tongue.
4. **Choking / Esophageal Obstruction:** Bolus of coarse fodder, whole root vegetables, or stones lodged in the esophagus.
5. **Toxicosis & Poisoning:** Organophosphate/carbamate agricultural pesticide ingestion, caustic fertilizer ingestion, poisonous weeds (e.g., Datura, Oleander, Lantana), or ingestion of moldy mycotoxic feed.
6. **Bluetongue Virus:** Edema of lips, cyanotic tongue, oral erosions, and fever.

## Clinical Symptoms & Signs
- Continuous ropy, stringy, or frothy saliva dripping from lips.
- "Champing" of jaws, teeth grinding, or frequent lip smacking.
- Reluctance or inability to chew (quidding: dropping partly chewed cud balls from mouth).
- Foul oral odor (halitosis) in cases of necrotic stomatitis or foreign body infection.
- Swollen lips, muzzle, or tongue with visible ulcerations, vesicles, or red bleeding patches.
- If toxicosis: muscle tremors, pin-point pupils, diarrhoea, staggering, or convulsions.
- If choking: distress, neck extension, coughing, and inability to swallow water.

## Veterinary Medical Treatment
- **Systematic Oral Examination:** A licensed veterinarian performs a careful speculum examination of the oral cavity, dental pads, tongue, and pharynx (wearing protective gloves).
- **Foreign Body Removal:** Careful extraction of lodged thorns, wires, or sharp grass seeds using forceps.
- **Disease-Specific Medical Therapy:**
  - If FMD / Viral Stomatitis: Symptomatic care with systemic broad-spectrum antibiotics (Oxytetracycline or Ceftriaxone) to control secondary infection; NSAIDs (Meloxicam) to alleviate oral pain; multivitamin injections (B-complex) to hasten epithelial healing.
  - If Pesticide / Chemical Toxicity: Immediate administration of specific antidotes (Atropine sulphate for organophosphate poisoning; 2-PAM under strict veterinary supervision); activated charcoal oral drench.
- **Topical Mouth Washes:** Antiseptic and astringent oral flushes with 1% potassium permanganate solution (pale pink), 2% sodium bicarbonate solution, or boroglycerin (5%) applied to tongue lesions twice daily.
- **Veterinary Warning:** Never reach deeply into the mouth without proper protective precautions, as infectious oral diseases and Rabies can cause salivation.

## Safe Supportive & Home Care
- **Visual Inspection Without Danger:** Inspect lips and front teeth from outside. Do not put bare hands deep inside the throat.
- **Gentle Oral Lavage:** Flush the outer mouth and muzzle gently with clean lukewarm saline water (1 teaspoon common salt in 1 liter clean warm water) or very light pink potassium permanganate solution.
- **Soft, Non-Abrasive Diet:** Offer soft, green, moist, palatable forage (tender lucerne, berseem, tender grass) or warm rice/wheat flour gruel. Remove all coarse, prickly dry straw, thorny acacia twigs, and hard grains that scratch ulcerated oral tissues.
- **Cool Clean Water:** Provide abundant fresh water in shallow clean buckets.
- **Isolation of Drooling Sheep:** If multiple sheep in the flock begin salivating simultaneously, isolate them immediately to prevent potential contagious viral spread (FMD/PPR).
- **Home Care Warning:** Home soothing rinses relieve mouth soreness; they do NOT cure toxicosis, choking, or viral diseases. Seek veterinary diagnostics immediately.

## Prevention & Management
- **Vaccination:** Routine biannual FMD vaccination and regular PPR / Bluetongue vaccination as advised by local animal husbandry departments.
- **Pasture Inspection:** Clear grazing fields and enclosures of sharp thorns, barbed wire scraps, and toxic weed patches.
- **Chemical Safety:** Store all agricultural pesticides, insecticides, urea, and chemical fertilizers in locked rooms far away from sheep grazing pens and water sources.
- **Feed Hygiene:** Never feed sheep moldy, blackened, or insect-damaged grains.

## Emergency Warning Signs
Call a veterinarian immediately if:
- Salivation is accompanied by rapid trembling, collapse, or twitching (pesticide poisoning).
- Sheep is gasping for breath with neck outstretched and cannot swallow (choking emergency).
- Oral ulcers are accompanied by high fever and severe lameness in multiple flock members (FMD alert).
