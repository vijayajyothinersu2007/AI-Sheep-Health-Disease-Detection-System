## Disease Overview
Hoof Wounds and Lameness in sheep comprise a spectrum of debilitating podiatric conditions including Foot Rot (infectious pododermatitis caused by synergistic infection of *Dichelobacter nodosus* and *Fusobacterium necrophorum*), interdigital dermatitis (scald), traumatic hoof puncture wounds, and secondary blowfly strike (myiasis / maggot wounds). Severe hoof disease causes intense pain, inability to graze, rapid body weight loss, reduced wool yield, and reproductive failure. It is exceptionally prevalent in high-rainfall seasons, muddy enclosures, and waterlogged pastures.

## Clinical Symptoms & Signs
- **Gait Abnormalities:** Reluctance to walk, severe head bobbing during walking, grazing on carpal joints ("kneeling" while feeding), or total recumbency.
- **Interdigital Inflammation:** Redness, swelling, and moist gray-white exudate in the skin between the claws (interdigital cleft).
- **Underrunning of Hoof Horn:** In virulent foot rot, the soft tissue of the sole separates from the hard outer wall, producing a characteristic putrid, foul, rotting odor.
- **Traumatic Punctures & Cracks:** Cracks in overgrown hooves, stones or thorns embedded in the sole, or puncture tracks leaking pus.
- **Maggot Wounds (Fly Strike / Myiasis):** Visible writhing fly larvae (maggots) feeding within infected hoof cavities, with bleeding, foul discharge, and extreme agitation.

## Transmission & Environmental Drivers
- *Dichelobacter nodosus* survives only 1-2 weeks in pasture soil, but can persist indefinitely inside chronic carrier sheep hooves.
- Wet, muddy conditions soften the interdigital skin, facilitating bacterial entry through minor abrasions.
- Transmission occurs rapidly when healthy sheep walk through the same muddy tracks, gathering pens, or wet bedding used by infected sheep.

## Veterinary Medical Treatment
- **Hoof Paring & Trimming:** Careful surgical paring of overgrown, loose, detached hoof horn by a veterinarian to expose bacteria to air and topical antiseptics (anaerobic bacteria are killed by oxygenation).
- **Foot Bath Therapy:** Group walking through medicated foot baths:
  - 10% Zinc Sulphate solution with surfactant, or
  - 5% Copper Sulphate solution, or
  - 2-5% Formalin solution.
  (Ensure sheep stand in foot bath for 3-5 minutes, then dry hooves on concrete before returning to pasture).
- **Systemic Antibiotic Therapy (Veterinary Prescription):** In severe or multi-limb cases, deep systemic injections of long-acting Oxytetracycline (20 mg/kg IM) or Procaine Penicillin are required.
- **Maggot Debridement:** Application of turpentine oil, chloroform, or veterinary ivermectin spray directly into maggot cavities to expel larvae, followed by povidone-iodine dressing.
- **Anti-inflammatory / Pain Relief:** Meloxicam under veterinary guidance to relieve severe orthopaedic pain and encourage feeding.

## Safe Supportive & Home Care
- **Clean and Dry Housing:** Move lame sheep immediately out of wet muddy pens into a completely dry, well-drained, clean shelter with dry straw or sand.
- **Debris Cleansing:** Wash the affected hooves gently with clean warm saline water or diluted potassium permanganate (light pink) using a clean brush to remove mud, dung, and stones.
- **Supportive Antiseptic Dressing:** Dust dry clean wounds with a safe mixture of boric acid and turmeric powder (Curcuma longa, a natural antiseptic) or apply zinc oxide ointment under vet advice.
- **Fly Repellent Protection:** Apply a thin layer of neem oil (*Azadirachta indica*) or herbal veterinary fly-repellent spray around the hoof to prevent flies from laying eggs.
- **Close Feeding Troughs:** Bring clean green fodder and fresh drinking water close to the sheep so it does not have to walk or suffer pain to feed.
- **Home Care Warning:** Home cleaning is supportive; advanced hoof rot with detached sole requires veterinary surgical paring and prescription footbath solutions.

## Prevention & Management
- **Routine Hoof Trimming:** Inspect and trim hooves 2 to 3 times per year (especially before the monsoon) using clean, sanitized hoof shears.
- **Dry Flooring & Drainage:** Ensure sheep pens have elevated wooden slatted floors or well-drained gravel/sand footing. Avoid stagnant water puddles near shed entrances.
- **Quarantine:** Isolate newly purchased sheep and inspect their hooves for foot rot odor or lesions for 21-30 days before flock integration.
- **Pasture Spelling:** Move healthy sheep onto pastures that have been rested (spelled) from livestock for at least 14 days to ensure environmental *Dichelobacter* die-off.

## Emergency Warning Signs
Seek prompt veterinary intervention if:
- Hoof shows active maggot infestation with deep cavitation and foul discharge.
- Sheep cannot stand or walk to water and food for > 24 hours.
- Severe swelling ascends up the leg above the fetlock joint (deep joint/tendon sheath sepsis).
