## Disease Overview
Peste des Petits Ruminants (PPR), also termed Small Ruminant Plague or Goat & Sheep Plague, is an acute, highly contagious viral disease of sheep and goats caused by a Morbillivirus of the family *Paramyxoviridae*. It is one of the most devastating infectious animal diseases globally, targeted by DAHD and FAO for complete eradication. PPR is characterized by severe pyrexia (fever), necrotizing erosive stomatitis, catarrhal to mucopurulent oculonasal discharge, severe necrotizing enteritis with foul watery-bloody diarrhoea, and bronchopneumonia. Morbidity can reach 90-100% and mortality 50-80% in susceptible flocks.

## Clinical Symptoms & Signs
- **Incubation Period:** 3 to 6 days.
- **High Fever:** Sudden onset of high fever (104°F - 106°F / 40°C - 41.5°C) with dullness, anorexia, and dry muzzle.
- **Oculonasal Catarrh:** Clear serous discharge from eyes and nostrils turning into thick, yellow mucopurulent exudate that encrusts and glues the eyelids and nostrils shut.
- **Necrotic Oral Lesions:** Gums, inner lips, dental pad, and underside of tongue show gray-white necrotic deposits and superficial erosions ("cooked meat" appearance), causing foul oral odor and drooling.
- **Severe Enteritis & Diarrhoea:** After 2-3 days of fever, severe, profuse, foul-smelling, greenish or bloody diarrhoea starts, leading to catastrophic dehydration, emaciation, and hypothermia.
- **Bronchopneumonia:** Coughing, abdominal laboured breathing, and rales. Death usually occurs within 5 to 10 days.

## Transmission & Spread
- Direct contact between infected and susceptible animals via aerosols, sneezed droplets, saliva, ocular and nasal discharges, and liquid feces.
- Shared water troughs, feed bunks, and livestock markets are major hotspots for rapid viral amplification.
- Animals recovering from natural infection possess life-long immunity.

## Veterinary Medical Treatment
- **Antiviral Status:** There is no specific curative antiviral medication for Morbillivirus.
- **Secondary Infection Control (Prescription Antibiotics):** Broad-spectrum antibiotics (Oxytetracycline, Enrofloxacin, or Ceftriaxone) administered by or under the direct guidance of a veterinarian to prevent fatal secondary pasteurella or bacterial pneumonia.
- **Symptomatic Antidiarrhoeals & Protectants:** Oral bismuth subnitrate, kaolin-pectin suspensions, or gut astringents under veterinary guidance.
- **Oral Antiseptic Care:** Flushing mouth with 1% potassium permanganate or applying boroglycerin to oral erosions.
- **Fluid & Electrolyte Resuscitation:** Subcutaneous or intravenous fluids (Dextrose Normal Saline, Ringer's Lactate) to counteract lethal dehydrating shock.
- **Veterinary Warning:** PPR is a scheduled notifiable disease in India. Any suspected outbreak must be reported to the local Government Veterinary Assistant Surgeon immediately.

## Safe Supportive & Home Care
- **Strict Isolation:** Isolate all suspected sheep immediately in a warm, dry quarantine shelter.
- **Clean Eyes & Nostrils:** Gently wipe crusted exudates from eyes and nostrils using sterile warm saline cotton swabs to restore vision and clear breathing passages.
- **Rehydration Gruel:** Offer warm water containing Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) or boiled rice water with jaggery and a pinch of salt.
- **Soft Green Feed:** Provide tender, non-abrasive green forage or mashed grain gruels.
- **Home Care Warning:** Home nursing provides vital hydration and comfort, but will NOT prevent mortality without veterinary intervention.

## Prevention & Vaccination
- **PPR Vaccine (Live Attenuated):** Administer 1 ml subcutaneously to all sheep and lambs above 3-4 months of age under the National Animal Disease Control Programme (NADCP). Provides solid immunity for at least 3 years (often life-long).
- **Movement Control:** Stop all animal movements and grazing on common lands during suspected local outbreaks.
- **Quarantine:** Maintain 30-day quarantine for all new flock additions.
- **Sanitization:** Disinfect infected premises with 2% sodium hydroxide or 1% Virkon-S.

## Emergency Warning Signs
Seek immediate emergency veterinary care if:
- Sheep shows high fever accompanied by eye/nasal crusting and foul watery diarrhoea.
- Rapid abdominal breathing and gasping with oral ulcerations.
- Rapid spread of similar signs across multiple flock members.
