## Disease Overview
Sheep Pox is a severe, highly contagious, debilitating viral infection of sheep caused by the Sheep Pox Virus (SPPV), belonging to the genus *Capripoxvirus* of the family *Poxviridae*. It is one of the most economically devastating sheep diseases in India and tropical countries, characterized by fever, widespread papular, nodular, and pustular skin lesions, and systemic respiratory and internal organ lesions. Mortality can reach 50% in adult sheep and up to 80-100% in young lambs.

## Clinical Symptoms & Signs
- **Early Stage (Prodromal):** Sudden high fever (104°F - 106°F or 40°C - 41.5°C), depression, accelerated respiration, anorexia (refusal to feed), swollen eyelids, and serous to mucopurulent nasal discharge.
- **Eruptive Skin Lesions:** Characteristic skin nodules and papules appear within 1 to 3 days, especially on hairless or sparsely wooled areas including:
  - Muzzle, lips, nostrils, and cheeks
  - Inner thighs, scrotum, and udder
  - Under surface of tail and perineum
- **Lesion Evolution:** Macules rapidly transform into firm round papules (0.5 to 1.5 cm diameter) surrounded by a red erythematous ring. These may become necrotic, forming hard, dark brown crusts (scabs) that leave scars upon shedding.
- **Respiratory Distress & Systemic Lesions:** Pox lesions also form in the lungs (pulmonary pox nodules), trachea, and abomasum, causing severe coughing, dyspnoea (laboured breathing), and fatal secondary pneumonia.

## Transmission & Spread
- Direct animal-to-animal contact through respiratory aerosols, nasal discharge, saliva, and dried scab dust.
- Indirect transmission via contaminated bedding, shearing tools, feed troughs, transport vehicles, and livestock markets.
- Mechanical transmission by biting flies (*Stomoxys calcitrans*) and ticks.
- Scabs and dry crusts shed by healing animals contain live virus that can persist in sheep sheds and dry soil for over 6 months.

## Veterinary Medical Treatment
- **Primary Clinical Principle:** There is no specific antiviral drug for Sheep Pox virus. Veterinary treatment focuses on aggressive supportive therapy and controlling secondary bacterial infections.
- **Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics (Veterinary Prescription Only):** Long-acting Oxytetracycline (20 mg/kg IM) or Enrofloxacin, administered strictly by or under the prescription of a registered veterinarian to prevent fatal secondary bacterial bronchopneumonia and skin sepsis.
- **Anti-inflammatory & Antipyretics:** Meloxicam or Flunixin meglumine under veterinary guidance to reduce high fever, pain, and respiratory discomfort.
- **Topical Wound Antiseptics:** Application of povidone-iodine solution (5%) or fly-repellent antiseptic spray to open skin erosions to prevent maggot infestation (myiasis) and bacterial infection.
- **Fluid & Electrolyte Support:** Oral rehydration or intravenous normal saline/dextrose under veterinary supervision for severely dehydrated or anorexic animals.
- **Veterinary Warning:** Never administer prescription antibiotics or inject drugs without professional veterinary diagnosis. Inappropriate dosage or unsterile injections can lead to death or drug resistance.

## Safe Supportive & Home Care
- **Immediate Isolation:** Isolate all affected sheep immediately in a well-ventilated, dry, clean quarantine enclosure separate from healthy flock members.
- **Soft Bedding & Sanitation:** Provide clean, dry straw bedding. Clean and disinfect pens daily using 2-3% sodium hydroxide (caustic soda) or bleaching powder wash.
- **Nutrition & Soft Forage:** Offer soft, moist, easily digestible green fodder (e.g., cowpea, berseem, tender grass) and warm gruel (rice water or wheat flour congee with a pinch of salt). The sheep cannot chew coarse dry roughage due to painful oral and lip pox lesions.
- **Hydration:** Ensure continuous access to clean, cool drinking water placed in shallow, easily reachable troughs. Add oral rehydration salts (ORS) or mild electrolytes to water.
- **Mild Mouth & Skin Cleansing:** Gently clean crusts around the mouth and nostrils with warm saline water or diluted potassium permanganate (1:1000 light pink solution) using clean sterile cotton. Do not peel off dry scabs forcefully as this causes deep bleeding wounds.
- **Home Care Warning:** Home care provides comfort and supportive hydration; it does NOT cure the viral infection or replace veterinary clinical care.

## Prevention & Vaccination
- **Routine Vaccination:** Live attenuated Sheep Pox vaccine administered subcutaneously annually (usually 0.5 ml per sheep) as per the state animal husbandry department schedule, preferably 1-2 months before anticipated seasonal outbreaks.
- **Ring Vaccination:** During an outbreak in adjacent villages, immediately vaccinate all healthy, unexposed sheep within a 5 km radius under veterinary direction. Do not vaccinate clinically sick animals.
- **Flock Biosecurity:** Quarantine all newly purchased sheep for at least 21 to 30 days before mixing with the main flock.
- **Vector Control:** Apply safe pyrethroid insecticidal dips or sprays around animal shelters to suppress biting fly populations.
- **Safe Carcass Disposal:** Infected sheep carcasses and shed scabs must be buried deep (at least 6 feet) with quicklime or completely incinerated. Never throw carcasses into open pastures or water bodies.

## Emergency Warning Signs
Seek immediate emergency veterinary care if:
- Rectal temperature exceeds 105°F (40.5°C) with severe shivering.
- Breathing becomes rapid, open-mouthed, and wheezing (pulmonary pox).
- Extensive purulent skin lesions develop with foul smell and maggots.
- Sheep collapses, becomes recumbent, and refuses all fluids for > 24 hours.
