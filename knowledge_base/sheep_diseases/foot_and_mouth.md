## Disease Overview
Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD), locally referred to as "Gali Kuntu" or "Komari", is an extremely contagious viral disease of cloven-hoofed livestock caused by an Aphthovirus of the family *Picornaviridae* (serotypes O, A, and Asia-1 in India). While in cattle FMD produces conspicuous blisters, in sheep the disease is often clinically insidious or mild, yet sheep act as major "maintenance hosts" transmitting the virus silently across wide areas. When severe, it produces sudden lameness, interdigital vesicles, coronary band detachment, oral erosions, and high mortality in young suckling lambs due to viral myocarditis ("tiger heart").

## Clinical Symptoms & Signs
- **Lameness (Dominant Sign in Sheep):** Sudden onset of severe lameness in multiple animals. The sheep walk with a stiff, painful gait or graze on their knees.
- **Coronary Band & Interdigital Lesions:** Blisters (vesicles) form on the skin-horn junction of the hooves, heel bulbs, and between claws. These rupture rapidly, leaving red, raw, painful ulcerations. In severe cases, the entire hoof horn may detach ("thimbling").
- **Mouth Lesions & Drooling:** Shallow erosions on the dental pad, hard palate, tongue, and gums. Lesions rupture within hours, producing stringy or ropy saliva and characteristic lip smacking.
- **Teat & Udder Lesions:** Ewes nursing lambs develop painful teat blisters, refusing to nurse, which results in acute starvation and death of young lambs.
- **Pyrexia:** Transient high fever (103°F to 105°F / 39.5°C to 40.5°C) and reduced milk yield.

## Transmission & Spread
- Direct aerosol transmission via exhaled breath, droplet contact, and contaminated milk, saliva, urine, and dung.
- Extremely high environmental stability: Virus can be carried on wind for kilometers under humid conditions.
- Mechanical transmission through clothing, transport vehicles, shepherd boots, and stray dogs.

## Veterinary Medical Treatment
- **Viral Status:** No specific antiviral drug exists. Treatment focuses on palliative wound care and prevention of crippling bacterial complications.
- **Antibiotic Coverage (Veterinary Prescription):** Systemic administration of broad-spectrum antibiotics (such as Oxytetracycline or Amoxicillin) to prevent secondary bacterial hoof infection, gangrenous mastitis, and fatal pneumonia.
- **Foot Disinfection & Care:** Flushing hooves with 4% sodium carbonate (washing soda) or 2% copper sulphate solution, followed by topical application of povidone-iodine and fly-repellent antiseptic sprays.
- **Oral Mouthwashes:** Gentle flushing of mouth lesions with 1% potassium permanganate solution (light pink) or 2% sodium bicarbonate solution.
- **Analgesics:** Meloxicam under veterinary guidance to relieve severe orthopaedic and oral pain.

## Safe Supportive & Home Care
- **Soft, Dry Enclosure:** Confine all affected sheep in dry, soft-bedded, shaded pens. Prevent walking over stony, rough, or muddy ground that tears healing hoof horn.
- **Feed Soft Fodder:** Provide soft green grass, cooked gruel, or tender leaves. Sheep with mouth ulcers cannot chew coarse dry straw.
- **Clean Water:** Provide ample clean drinking water within easy reach.
- **Care for Lambs:** If nursing ewes have teat lesions, assist-feed newborn lambs with boiled, cooled goat milk or warm cow milk using a clean sanitized bottle.
- **Home Care Warning:** Home nursing eases pain; FMD is a highly infectious statutory disease requiring immediate notification to local veterinary authorities.

## Prevention & Vaccination
- **Biannual Vaccination:** Mandatory routine FMD vaccination under the National Animal Disease Control Programme (NADCP) twice a year (every 6 months) for all sheep above 3-4 months of age.
- **Flock Quarantine:** Strict 21-day quarantine of newly introduced animals.
- **Biosecurity Footbaths:** Place lime powder or 4% washing soda footbaths at farm entry points.

## Emergency Warning Signs
- Sudden death of young suckling lambs without preceding signs (viral myocarditis).
- Multiple sheep unable to stand or severe hoof detachment with deep bacterial infection.
