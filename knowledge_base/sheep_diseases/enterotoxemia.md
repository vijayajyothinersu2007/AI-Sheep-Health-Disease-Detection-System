## Disease Overview
Pulpy Kidney Disease, also known as Enterotoxemia or Overeating Disease, is an acute, peracute, highly lethal toxaemia of sheep and lambs caused by *Clostridium perfringens* type D. The bacterium normally resides in low numbers in the intestines without causing harm. When sheep consume sudden excess starch, cereal grains, rich lush pasture, or sudden changes in feed, undigested carbohydrates pass into the intestine. This triggers explosive bacterial proliferation and the production of lethal Epsilon toxin. The toxin causes massive vascular permeability, systemic oedema, convulsions, rapid death, and characteristic post-mortem pulpy softening of the kidneys.

## Clinical Symptoms & Signs
- **Peracute Cases:** Often, healthy, best-conditioned fast-growing lambs or sheep are found dead in the morning without any prior sign of sickness.
- **Acute Cases:**
  - Dullness, depression, and complete loss of appetite.
  - Neurological Signs: Staggering gait, head pressing against walls, blindness, teeth grinding, lateral recumbency, opisthotonos (head retracted arched backward over spine).
  - Violent clonic convulsions with paddling movements of limbs and frothing at the mouth.
  - Hyperesthesia (extreme sensitivity to touch or sound).
  - Subnormal body temperature during terminal stage, respiratory distress, and death within 2 to 12 hours.
- **Subacute Cases (Older Sheep):** Greenish-watery diarrhoea, severe abdominal pain (colic), lethargy, and progressive weakness.

## Root Causes & Diet Overload
- Sudden shift from dry roughage to lush green pasture (especially after monsoon or irrigation).
- Sudden heavy feeding of cereal grains (maize, jowar, wheat, rice) or concentrate without gradual conditioning.
- Heavy gastrointestinal worm infestation (*Haemonchus contortus*) causing sluggish intestinal motility (stasis), allowing toxins to accumulate and absorb.
- Lack of routine pre-monsoon vaccination.

## Veterinary Medical Treatment
- **Emergency Nature:** Because death occurs extremely rapidly from circulating epsilon toxin, medical treatment is often difficult unless detected in the earliest stage.
- **Specific Antiserum Therapy:** Administration of *Clostridium perfringens* Type D hyperimmune antiserum (if available at veterinary clinics) can neutralize circulating toxin.
- **Antibiotic Therapy:** Parenteral Penicillin G or Amoxicillin, combined with oral administration of non-absorbable intestinal antibiotics (such as Sulfadimidine or Metronidazole) under strict veterinary direction to suppress proliferating intestinal clostridia.
- **Sedation & Anticonvulsants:** Diazepam or Meloxicam to control violent convulsions and relieve distress.
- **Oral Neutralizers:** Oral dosing with activated charcoal or magnesium oxide suspension under veterinary guidance to bind toxins and neutralize rumen acidosis.
- **Veterinary Warning:** Do not delay veterinary intervention. Overeating disease cannot be cured by household oils; veterinary assessment is urgent.

## Safe Supportive & Home Care
- **Immediate Diet Halt:** Immediately withdraw all cereal grains, rich concentrates, commercial pellets, and lush green fodder.
- **Fiber Reintroduction:** Provide exclusively clean, dry roughage (dry jowar stover, sorghum straw, or clean dry grass hay). Dry fiber stimulates rumination, gut motility, and helps flush out toxins.
- **Hydration:** Provide fresh, clean drinking water with mild oral rehydration salts (ORS) or baking soda (sodium bicarbonate: 1 teaspoon in 1 liter of water under vet advice) to counteract acidosis.
- **Gentle Rumen Stimulation:** Ensure the animal remains warm and dry. If recumbent, position the sheep in sternal recumbency (upright on its chest) with soft straw bedding to avoid bloat and aspiration.
- **Home Care Warning:** Home care is strictly limited to diet correction. Any neurological signs (circling, head pressing) require urgent veterinary care.

## Prevention & Vaccination
- **Vaccination (Primary Defense):**
  - Enterotoxemia (ET) multicomponent vaccine (often combined with Tetanus / Lamb Dysentery).
  - Administer 1st dose at 2-3 months of age, booster dose 3-4 weeks later, and annual booster 2-4 weeks before expected lush pasture or seasonal concentrate feeding.
  - Pregnant ewes must be vaccinated 4-6 weeks prior to lambing to transfer colostral protective antibodies to newborn lambs.
- **Gradual Feeding Transition (The 14-Day Rule):**
  - Never change feed abruptly.
  - When introducing grains, concentrates, or silage, start with 50g per day and increase slowly over 10-14 days until full target ration is reached.
  - Never allow hungry sheep sudden access to harvested crop fields (maize, wheat stubble) where fallen grain can be gorged.
- **Strategic Deworming:** Deworm the entire flock before feed transitions to prevent gut sluggishness.

## Emergency Warning Signs
Seek emergency veterinary care immediately if:
- Sheep exhibits head pulling backwards (opisthotonos), teeth grinding, or blind circling.
- Violent leg paddling or sudden collapse with frothing at the mouth occurs.
- Multiple well-fed sheep suddenly develop severe watery diarrhoea and colic.
