## Clinical Philosophy & Safety Warning
**CRITICAL NOTICE:** All scheduled veterinary medications, injectables, prescription antibiotics, and anthelmintics MUST be prescribed, calculated, and confirmed by a licensed veterinarian. Uncontrolled, unverified, or lay administration of antibiotics causes fatal toxicity, treatment failure, and rapid emergence of antimicrobial resistance (AMR). Never guess drug dosages. This guide provides educational information regarding therapeutic categories and veterinary clinical principles.

## Therapeutic Categories in Sheep Practice

### 1. Broad-Spectrum Antibiotics (Prescription Only)
- **Oxytetracycline (Long-Acting 200 mg/ml):**
  - *Class:* Tetracycline antibiotic.
  - *Purpose & Use:* Primary drug of choice in small ruminant field practice for controlling secondary bacterial infections in Sheep Pox, Foot Rot, Chlamydia abortion, Pasteurellosis, and bacterial pneumonia.
  - *Mechanism:* Bacteriostatic; inhibits bacterial protein synthesis via the 30S ribosomal subunit.
  - *Precautions:* Strict deep intramuscular injection. Avoid rapid intravenous administration (causes cardiac collapse). Do not administer concurrently with bactericidal penicillins.
- **Enrofloxacin (100 mg/ml):**
  - *Class:* Fluoroquinolone.
  - *Purpose & Use:* Severe gram-negative respiratory infections (Pasteurella, Mycoplasma), severe coliform enteritis, and complicated skin sepsis.
  - *Mechanism:* Bactericidal; inhibits bacterial DNA-gyrase.
  - *Precautions:* Contraindicated in very young growing lambs due to articular cartilage damage risk.
- **Procaine Penicillin & Benzathine Penicillin:**
  - *Class:* Beta-lactam.
  - *Purpose & Use:* Clostridial infections (*Clostridium perfringens* / Pulpy Kidney, Tetanus, Blackleg) and foot rot.
  - *Precautions:* Anaphylactic risk in sensitized animals. Never inject intravenously.
- **Sulfadimidine (33.3% Solution):**
  - *Class:* Sulfonamide.
  - *Purpose & Use:* Haemorrhagic Septicaemia (Domma), acute coccidiosis, and enteric infections.
  - *Precautions:* Ensure abundant hydration to prevent renal sulfonamide crystalluria.

### 2. Anti-inflammatory & Analgesic Drugs (NSAIDs)
- **Meloxicam (5 mg/ml):**
  - *Class:* Preferential COX-2 inhibitor NSAID.
  - *Purpose & Use:* Alleviates pain, high fever (antipyretic), and swelling in acute lameness, Sheep Pox lesions, throat inflammation in Domma, and mastitis.
  - *Precautions:* Do not use in severely dehydrated animals or those with renal impairment.
- **Flunixin Meglumine:**
  - *Class:* Potent non-steroidal anti-inflammatory.
  - *Purpose & Use:* Rapid control of endotoxic shock, acute pulmonary inflammation, and visceral pain in peracute bacterial infections.

### 3. Anthelmintics (Dewormers)
- **Albendazole / Fenbendazole:**
  - *Class:* Benzimidazoles.
  - *Purpose & Use:* Broad-spectrum control of adult and larval gastrointestinal roundworms (*Haemonchus*, *Trichostrongylus*), lungworms, and tapeworms.
  - *Precautions:* Avoid high doses in the first month of pregnancy (potential teratogenic risk).
- **Ivermectin (1% SC):**
  - *Class:* Macrocyclic lactone.
  - *Purpose & Use:* Effective against gastrointestinal nematodes, lungworms, and ectoparasites (mange mites, sucking lice, nasal bots).

### 4. Topical Antiseptics & Wound Care
- **Povidone-Iodine Solution & Ointment (5%):** Safe topical antiseptic for skin ulcers, pox lesions, and post-trimming hoof wounds.
- **Potassium Permanganate (1:1000 dilution):** Mild astringent antiseptic flush for mouth lesions in FMD, Orf, and stomatitis.
- **Zinc Oxide & Boric Acid Powder:** Drying protective powder for moist eczema and interdigital hoof scald.
- **Herbal Fly-Repellent Wound Sprays (containing Neem, Pine oil, Camphor):** Safe application to prevent fly strike (maggots) on wounds.

### 5. Fluid & Electrolyte Therapy
- **Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS):** Essential first-line supportive treatment for sheep suffering from diarrhoea, fever, or anorexia to restore fluid and electrolyte balance.
- **Dextrose Normal Saline (DNS) & Ringer's Lactate (IV):** Administered by veterinarians in severe dehydration, shock, and toxaemia.
