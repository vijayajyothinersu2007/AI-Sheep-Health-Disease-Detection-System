## Fundamental Boundary: Home Care vs Veterinary Treatment vs Emergency Care

| Care Level | Definition | Appropriate Interventions | When to Escalate |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **🏠 Safe Supportive Home Care** | Non-invasive nursing, comfort, sanitation, and dietary adjustments that aid recovery. | Clean dry bedding, shade, isolation, soft palatable green feed, clean water, oral rehydration (ORS), gentle saline cleaning of crusted skin or hooves, natural fly repellents. | If animal stops drinking, fever > 104°F, or condition worsens over 24h. |
| **🩺 Veterinary Clinical Care** | Clinical diagnosis, prescription medications, parenteral injections, surgery, and specific vaccinations. | Injectable antibiotics, NSAIDs, anthelmintics, surgical hoof paring, antitoxin serum, IV catheterization. | Required for all systemic bacterial infections, viral disease complications, and severe lameness. |
| **🚨 Emergency Triage** | Life-saving immediate clinical intervention. | Airway resuscitation for throat choking, shock fluids, atropine for toxicity, emergency bloat trocharization. | Immediate emergency veterinary hospital call; delay results in death within hours. |

## Scientifically Supported Safe Home Practices
1. **Turmeric (*Curcuma longa*) & Neem (*Azadirachta indica*) Powder for Superficial Wounds:**
   - *Application:* Clean superficial abrasions or dry hoof cuts can be dusted with a clean mixture of fine dry turmeric powder and clean wood ash, or coated with pure neem oil.
   - *Evidence:* Curcumin possesses documented mild antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties, while azadirachtin acts as a natural fly repellent to reduce maggot strike.
   - *Precaution:* Never pack deep, bleeding, or puncture wounds with contaminated dung or crude herbs.
2. **Mild Warm Saline Rinse for Mouth & Muzzle Lesions:**
   - *Application:* Dissolve 1 flat teaspoon of clean common salt in 1 liter of boiled, cooled water. Use clean cotton to gently dab away crusted nasal discharge and outer lip crusts.
   - *Evidence:* Isotonic saline softens dried crusts without stinging, restores tissue hydration, and inhibits surface bacterial growth.
3. **Rice Congee / Flour Gruel for Weak or Sick Sheep:**
   - *Application:* Boil broken rice or wheat flour in water into a thin, easily drinkable gruel with a pinch of salt and jaggery (molasses). Cool to room temperature before feeding.
   - *Evidence:* Provides readily digestible energy and prevents hypoglycemia without causing rumen acidosis when sheep cannot chew solid roughage.
4. **Hydration with Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS):**
   - *Application:* Standard WHO formula ORS or clean homemade solution (1 liter boiled water + 1 level teaspoon salt + 4 level teaspoons sugar/jaggery).
   - *Evidence:* Crucial for sheep with diarrhoea or fever to prevent fatal dehydration.

## Harmful or Unproven Folk Practices (STRICTLY FORBIDDEN)
- ❌ **Do NOT use hot iron branding:** Traditional branding of the face, muzzle, or joints causes third-degree burns, severe sepsis, shock, and intense suffering without therapeutic benefit.
- ❌ **Do NOT pour engine oil, kerosene, or battery acid into wounds:** Highly toxic, causes tissue necrosis, and risks systemic organ failure.
- ❌ **Do NOT forcefully drench a gasping animal:** Drenching liquids down the throat of a breathless or convulsing sheep causes fluid to enter the lungs, producing immediate fatal aspiration pneumonia.
- ❌ **Do NOT use dirty rags or dung to stop bleeding:** Extremely high risk of lethal Tetanus (*Clostridium tetani*) infection.
